How it worked

Two-thirds of the average: also a fair description of our completion percentage.

Level-k thinking: how many steps ahead?

Level 0 picks without thinking: on average, 50.

Level 1 thinks everyone else is Level 0, and best-responds: ⅔ × 50 ≈ 33.

Level 2 thinks everyone else is Level 1: ⅔ × 33 ≈ 22.

Level k guesses 50 × (⅔)k. Keep going forever and you reach 0.

Michigan's defense also operates at Level 0: it assumes the other team will do something random.

Nash equilibrium: keep eliminating

Press → to eliminate another round of strategies. Michigan's playoff chances were eliminated in round one.

So why didn't we all say zero?

Zero is right only if everyone is rational, knows everyone is rational, knows that everyone knows… all the way down. That's common knowledge of rationality, and it's a tall order.
Winning ≠ being "rational." The winner is whoever best predicts how many steps the room takes. The best guess is one step deeper than everyone else, and no deeper.
Keynes saw it in 1936: picking stocks is like a newspaper beauty contest, where you win by choosing the face others will think is prettiest. Markets reward anticipating average opinion.
Play it again and guesses fall toward zero. Equilibrium is where learning ends up, not where it starts.

Steal this game (please)

What it is

Two web pages and a tiny server: a projector deck that runs the round, and a phone page students reach by QR code. No app, no logins, no LMS.

How it was built

Node + Express, guesses held in memory, phones checking in every two seconds. Hosted free on Render, deployed by pushing to GitHub. Written in an afternoon with an AI coding assistant.

Why it works in class

Everyone plays before they learn. The theory then explains their own data, so Nash equilibrium becomes a story about the room, not a definition.

Make it yours

Change ⅔ to ½ or 1.1. Add a second round. Try a public-goods game, a market for lemons, an auction, a guess-the-CPI contest. Same skeleton.

Hail to the victors! And to every instructor willing to let the class run the experiment.
